# Doubling of the Earth's Population Creates an Investment Opportunity in the Contraceptive Industry

If the world were to double in population, specifically leaving the current age distribution unchanged, I would invest $210M in the contraceptive market. To sustain Earth's population, governments will need to make major changes to birth policy, and I would target investment into Karex's condom manufacturing, Thai Rubber Latex Group for resource control, and Cupid Limited for institutional sales infrastructure.

For the sake of this discussion, it's important to base this situation around several key assumptions:

1. The people who suddenly appear in our world will be relatively similar to us. For instance, a 50-year-old will generally be wiser than a 5-year-old, and all will have some common sense about the world around them. Additionally, skilled labor will remain with the same distribution as before.
2. Money doubles. To avoid any massive imbalance in wealth, the doubled population will maintain the same wealth distribution. This will come with a myriad of fiscal issues; intense inflation and an upside-down labor market, namely, but we will assume finances will stabilize.
3. Billions of people will die. The population will double, from ~8.2 billion to 16.4 billion.[^1] Right now, the global food supply makes enough food for 2700 calories per person per day, yet as many as 800 million people today suffer from chronic undernutrition. When our population doubles, the number of starving people will be estimated at 3.28 billion.[^2]

## Strict Birth Controls Are Nothing New; Governments Will Likely Take Control

Contraceptives are the primary solution to bringing the population back to sustainable levels, and limiting growth after this event will be a top concern. Food scarcity, clean water, and housing prices will also be profit-ripe issues, but the biggest shift will be in how we view having children globally, and the contraceptive industry can scale quickly to help governments handle this cultural shift.

Market dynamics will shift from contraceptives as a discretionary product to a government-subsidized instrument for stabilizing economies. In a resource-constrained world, population management cannot be left to consumer choice; it will be driven by state-level monopsonies, which historically have fallen back on aggressive control policies. China's One-Child Policy and India's mass sterilization campaigns illustrate the scale and precedent for this kind of state-enforced demographic containment.

In 1983 alone, China carried out 20.7 million sterilizations, 17.8 million IUD insertions, and 14.4 million abortions, most of which were involuntary, with the program only discontinued in 2016.[^3] In 1975, India suspended civil liberties and sterilized 6.2 million men in a single year. However, with an instantaneous population doubling, no country has the infrastructure to carry out a sterilization campaign, nor would it likely be wise to sterilize so many people for a myriad of reasons: human rights, long-term viability of humanity, and civil unrest. In the short term, governments will turn to quickly produced, reversible contraception, with condoms being the most widely utilized.[^4]

## Investment into Three Pieces of Condom Manufacturing Will Lead to Growth, but Will Be Challenging to Accurately Value

To capture value in this space, I will start by acquiring Thai Rubber Latex Group,[^5] a Thailand-based rubber manufacturer with a $26M market cap trading at 0.3x book value. Offering $30M (a ~20% premium) secures the company and ample room for returns, along with access to 4.3 billion THB in manufacturing PPE. This serves as an upstream hedge against rising input costs in condom manufacturing, captures demand for other rubber-dependent products like nitrile gloves, insulates Karex from supply-chain bottlenecks, and opens a secondary revenue stream by supplying competitors and adjacent industries. An additional $50 million will be invested into the expansion of factories and manufacturing processes in anticipation of skyrocketing demand for rubber products, although it is unlikely that Thai Rubber Latex Group will be able to cover all of Karex's manufacturing.

Karex, headquartered in Malaysia, is the world's largest condom manufacturer, accounting for 20% of the global market and dominating Asia, a primary demand target given the precedent for sterilization government mandates there. As of July 3, 2026, its market cap is $125M USD. I will purchase the firm for $150M (a 20% premium), plus an additional $20M for factory expansion. Karex manufactures for major third-party brands like Trojan and Durex, which largely outsource — an expensive position when resources are strained. Karex will shift its consumer focus from retail to institutional clients: governments, the WHO, and the UN.

Cupid Limited, based in India with a ~$2.8B market cap, is a niche player with male and female offerings and was the first company to receive WHO pre-qualification. I will partner with them, primarily targeting their institutional networks like UNFPA, South Africa's Ministry of Health, Tanzania's Medical Stores Department, and Brazil's Ministry of Health.[^6] Combining these three firms opens sovereign contracts while leveraging Karex's high-volume manufacturing. Cupid's product diversification offers marginal additional value beyond the male condom, but strong synergies include vertical integration, combined R&D, increased market share, greater bargaining power, and economies of scale and quality.

In modeling profitability, I will evaluate return in today's dollars. Product prices will skyrocket with the doubled money supply, but modeling monetary policy and market dynamics with precision is difficult. To model revenue and margins as the addressable market doubles, I will start with baseline revenue figures, compressed gross margins, and long-term market stabilization.

In the attached Excel appendix, I arrived at a rough valuation of $1.14B by building a baseline model with AI and tweaking assumptions to deliver a conservative return. Revenue peaks in phase 3 once our supply chain catches up to demand, and our consumer base shifts back to retail consumers. Government contracting will be critical to staying afloat in the early phases, but, by nature, is a lower-margin customer. By phase 3, our business will have stabilized at $2B in total revenue, driven by strong market share and solid rubber manufacturing. Investment in Karex factories and Thai Rubber Latex production will allow us to scale more quickly than competitors. The total investment in all firms will amount to $250M, and diverting 5% of profits during our partnership with Cupid will leave the total value of this investment after the doubling event at ~$800B, a 320% return.
