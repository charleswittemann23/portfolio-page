I’ve begun reading Benjamin Graham’s The Intelligent Investor, after 
hearing repeated references to it in John Bogle’s Little Book of Common 
Sense Investing.  While I appreciate John Bogle’s more recent analysis and 
references, I can’t help but admit that Graham was on the right track long 
before so many others, which is even more remarkable. 

I could liken it to being an early follower of Jesus Christ. Bogle’s steadfast 
allegiance to low-cost index funds and “boring” investing is impressive, especially 
given that, for much of the time, he was a lonely advocate for this approach. However, 
it’s even more impressive that Graham was so on the nose with his characterization of 
what wise investing will look like in an era where quite literally no one else had discussed 
concepts like this. Warren Buffett describes this as the best investing book, in part because 
it really is revolutionary, but I think especially given when it was written.
