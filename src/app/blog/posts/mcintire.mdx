## Short Answer
Mmmm, maybe. There are many determining factors. Like with most things in my life, I regret little, 
but I feel strong that given another chance, I would apply once again.

### Social Aspect
Coming from a computer science background, the commerce social experience was a marked improvement on my social life in class.
Let me paint a picture: 100 person lecture hall, computer screens out, and a general stench of B.O. that fills the air. Now, in McIntire,
one certaintly might struggle with the lack of sunlight; majority of classrooms don't have any windows. But, people talk to one another!
Part of this is do with improved social skills, but another part of this stems from a huge participation grade!

### My advancemend as a lifelong learner

Like with probably every department, quality of teaching ebbs and flows with your professor. Professor D and D, 
I appreciate your ineptitude (or at best, inability to resonate with most students), but altogether, the quality of experience
at McIntire is differentiating. I'll spend some time touching on ICE specifically, but there are a strong number of excellent professors. 
1. A number of professors come into academia with significant experience in industry, and not only that, but a considerably strong
background in their field. Speaking specifically to finance, the number of hedge fund managers, successful upper management of bulge banks, or
even the former head of the UVA endowment(I will take a class with him next semester). Sometimes in other instances, academia is a space for 
professionals who don't make it in industry. There certaintly are some of those people, but a majority
of professors either greatly enjoy teaching, or greatly appreciate the opportunity to do research. Additionally, I would also
relate strong industry success to being  a complete professor, which is something I can't say about others in non-business contexts. 
A strong programmer often times is a terrible teacher. A successful businessman often is an excellent
communicator and deeply relational. 
2. Professors encourage processes not outcomes. This was something I've heard before, and believe to be true, 
but rarely have I seen it executed well. A principle example would be our class discussions, which I quickly learned wouldn't be reaching a correct answer,
but much more a thought experiment. I most appreciated the professors who would push back on ideas, and challenge our ideas. With *TRIGGER WARNING... AI becoming increasingly
competent at generating outcomes, being taught how to examine thought critically was something I appreciated.
3. Generally, the faculty makes themselves available. Honestly, not sure. I wanted to come up with a third point. Statistically,
in national rankings, McIntire comes out high based on faculty rankings, so that in essence is what my point will boil down to.

## ICE
Most of my frustration with ICE will be said later, or has already been said. Having an engaged group during the Fall
dramatically improves the experience. The experience is very block dependent. It seems as though they are making some changes.
QA, marketing are a joke. The semester deliverable was a valuable experience, and I derived a lot of satisfaction
from our presentation and completion.

### Grading
Arguably the most frustrating part of McIntire(Exception: Markets Bootcamp, I secretly loved 
the normalization of all grades). Naturally, everyone in McIntire is quite hard working, and it encouraged
me to work hard at first to deliver excellent work. But I quickly realized all work gets curved to an A-/B+.
From an economic standpoint, the effort required to guarantee an A is a negative utility outcome.
Grade outcomes have become less important to me. Perhaps there is some lesson here about 
how in business, not being first is being last. Or how the 1% truly work so much harder than the rest. Or that we shouldn't focus on outcomes(see point 2.)?
Maybe there is some wisdom in their policy, but it is incredibly frustrating, and I know is a demotivator for many.

### Career Outcomes

Certainly worth consideration. You will be around many people that are highly driven, connected, and generally easy to work with. 
In any given class, you become aware quickly of highly intelligent people based on how they participate in lecture. The Career
Center claims to be helpful, but I have not been personally made aware of its successes by other students. 
Generally, I would say many of the students coming to McIntire were already going places, and McIntire 
benefits from having those students. More clearly said, McIntire doesn't create incredible career outcomes,
but if you get into McIntire, you are likely one of the students more likely to be successful.
It does have a solid brand reputation, and place well into investment banking. I suppose that is worth something.
